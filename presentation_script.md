# 5-Minute Presentation Script

Good morning everyone. Today I am presenting **ReWear Blocks**, a fictional LEGO x Patagonia collaboration built around one simple idea: repair should not feel like a compromise. It should feel creative, visible, and worth sharing.

The industry challenge is that many people already understand sustainability, but their daily behavior does not always follow that belief. When a jacket sleeve tears, a backpack corner wears out, or school gear looks old, replacement often feels easier than repair. The problem is not only technical. It is emotional and behavioral. Repair can feel boring, messy, or even embarrassing. ReWear Blocks turns that weakness into an opportunity by making repair modular, colorful, and expressive.

This opportunity matters because it creates value on several levels. For customers, it extends the life of clothing and gear. For communities, it creates a hands-on climate action activity that families, students, and outdoor users can actually participate in. For the brands, it opens a new product line with starter kits, refill packs, seasonal color drops, and repair workshops. LEGO contributes the language of modular building and play. Patagonia contributes credibility around durable products, repair culture, and environmental responsibility.

Our clear problem statement is this: sustainability-aware families, students, and casual outdoor users need a simple and attractive way to repair damaged clothing and gear, because existing repair options often feel too difficult, too plain, or too disconnected from personal identity. The key pain points are low repair confidence, lack of convenient materials, and the feeling that repaired items may look worse than before. Their needs are guidance, durable materials, and a design system that makes the repair look intentional.

To avoid assuming these needs are only our imagination, the MVP should be validated directly. We would run user interviews with people who own damaged clothing or bags, collect a short repair diary to understand what they avoid fixing, host concierge repair workshops, and test landing-page signups or preorders before scaling production. The goal is to prove that people not only like the idea, but are willing to use it, pay for it, and share the result.

The product vision is to make repair a creative ritual. The MVP is the **ReWear Blocks Repair Kit**. It includes 24 modular recycled-fabric patches, repair thread, a pocket-size guide, and a QR code that opens short tutorials for different damage types. The modular patch system solves the need for attractive repair by letting users combine colors and shapes. The QR tutorials solve the confidence gap by showing how to repair tears, elbows, knees, straps, and backpack corners. The workshop model solves the motivation gap by making sustainability social and hands-on.

Compared with basic patch packs, sewing kits, or professional repair services, ReWear Blocks occupies a different position. Basic patches are affordable, but often not guided or expressive. Professional repair can be high quality, but it removes the DIY learning moment and may feel slow or expensive. ReWear Blocks sits between practical repair and creative customization. It gives people a product they can use themselves, while also giving them a reason to feel proud of the visible result.

To test market feedback, I would start with a small pilot: 300 MVP kits distributed through two outdoor stores and one school or community workshop. We would measure signups, purchase conversion, repair completion, tutorial scans, and whether users upload or share their repaired item. We would also test pricing across a starter kit, refill pack, and workshop bundle.

The MVP is successful if users voluntarily complete repairs and want the system to continue. The proposed success metrics are: 70 percent of workshop participants complete at least one repair, 40 percent of kit buyers scan a tutorial within 14 days, 30 percent express interest in refill patches, and 25 percent share or upload their repaired item for impact tracking.

In conclusion, ReWear Blocks is not just a patch kit. It is a business concept that changes how repair feels. It transforms damage into design, sustainability into participation, and product life extension into something visible and desirable. The next step is to build the small-batch MVP, run the pilot, and use real customer behavior to decide how far the concept should scale.
